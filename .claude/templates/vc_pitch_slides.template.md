The skill `.claude/skills/vc_pitch.write_slides/SKILL.md` holds the deck flow
and the pitfalls of each slide

# 1. Elevator Pitch

- Question: what does the company do, for whom, and how does it make money?
- Content:
  - The problem, with 2-3 example personas who feel it
  - The solution in 3 verbs: find, run, deliver
  - The business model: price and unit
  - The edge in one line
  - Traction in one line, when the deck has no Traction slide

# 2. Problem

- Question: is the pain real, big, and urgent?
- Content:
  - The problem, with one customer persona as an example
  - The pain in rates, hours, dollars, or risk, each with a source
  - The skills and costs that make the status quo hard

# 3. Solution and Product

- Question: how does the company remove the pain, and does it work?
- Content:
  - How customers cope today
  - With the company: the user flow in 3 numbered steps, each with an example
  - One simple diagram of how it works
  - At most 4 advantages, as customer outcomes, not features

# 4. Traction

- Question: do real customers use, pay, or commit?
- Content:
  - Commercial metrics first: revenue, paying customers, growth rate, retention
  - Without commercial metrics: the strongest usage signal (prototype, pilots,
    letters of intent, waitlist)
  - The observed funnel, when the product has one
  - A value and a date for every metric
  - The launch date of the production product

# 5. Market Size

- Question: is the market big enough to return the fund?
- Content:
  - Bottom-up estimate: customers x units x price
  - TAM, SAM, and SOM, each with currency, time unit, and year
  - The beachhead segment inside SOM
  - A top-down analyst figure only as a cross-check

# 6. Competition

- Question: who else solves this, and where does the company sit?
- Content:
  - 3-5 real alternatives, including the status quo, with prices
  - A table on 2-4 criteria that the customer cares about, or a 2x2 grid on the
    business model (e.g., who does the work, who bears the outcome risk)
  - The company's position, with one price comparison

# 7. Why Now, Moat, and IP

- Question: why is the market open today, and why does the company keep winning?
- Content:
  - 2-3 dated trends that opened the market: technology, regulation, behavior,
    cost curve
  - The durable edge: 3 advantages that reinforce each other (data, technology,
    process, distribution, IP)

# 8. \<Claim\>, Not \<Objection\> (Optional)

- Question: what is the main investor objection, and why does it not apply?
- Content:
  - The claim that sets the product apart, and how it works
  - The objection (e.g., "AI spam"), and why the company is not it
  - A table of the sides involved, what each wins, and what enforces it
  - The result for the business

# 9. Business Model and Financials

- Question: how does the company make money, and where does the business go?
- Content:
  - Who pays, for what, how much, and how often
  - The billable unit, and the cost of the in-house alternative
  - Unit economics: price, cost, gross margin, LTV to CAC
  - The main cost driver in plain English, with a sensitivity table
  - The key assumptions, then the revenue path for 3 years

# 10. Go-to-Market (GTM) Strategy

- Question: how does the company win its first customers and then scale?
- Content:
  - Beachhead: the first segment, its buyer, channel, and sales motion
  - 3 phases with a time window and a numeric milestone each
  - Target CAC and payback period, here or in the business model assumptions

# 11. Team

- Question: why is this team the one to win?
- Content:
  - Each founder: name, role, one credential related to the problem
  - First hires and advisors
  - Known gaps and the plan to fill them

# 12. The Ask

- Question: how much money, what does it buy, and what do investors get?
- Content:
  - Amount, round, runway in months, and net burn
  - 2-3 numbered milestones the round reaches
    - The last milestone justifies the next round
  - Use of funds, with shares that sum to 100% and amounts
  - Why invest: 3 reasons, each backed by a slide
